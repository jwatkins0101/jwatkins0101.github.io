Section A: Implementation Go/No-Go Framework

Structured decision-making for post-Phase 2 investment decisions

Purpose

At the end of Phase 2, the PE fund needs to decide: Should we fund implementation of these AI opportunities? This framework provides a structured recommendation.

The 6-Dimension Scoring Model (Phase 2)

Score each dimension 1-5 based on Phase 2 findings:

1. PROTOTYPE PERFORMANCE (1-5)

Did the working prototype demonstrate real value?

ScoreCriteria
5Prototype exceeded expectations; users immediately saw value; requested expansion
4Prototype met expectations; clear path to production; minor refinements needed
3Prototype showed promise but significant gaps; 50/50 on production viability
2Prototype underperformed; major technical or adoption barriers discovered
1Prototype failed; fundamental flaws in approach or technology
Key Evidence: User feedback during demo | Accuracy/quality of outputs | Processing time vs. manual baseline | Error rates observed

2. ECONOMIC VALIDATION (1-5)

Do the numbers hold up under scrutiny?

ScoreCriteria
5Economics stronger than Phase 1 estimates; additional savings discovered
4Economics validated within 10% of Phase 1 estimates; assumptions confirmed
3Economics partially validated; 20-30% variance from estimates; some uncertainty
2Economics weaker than estimated; 40%+ variance; key assumptions disproven
1Economics don't work; negative or marginal ROI even in best case
Key Evidence: Actual time studies vs. estimates | Volume data validated | Cost inputs confirmed | Realization factors adjusted based on findings

3. TECHNICAL FEASIBILITY (1-5)

Can this actually be built and deployed?

ScoreCriteria
5Straightforward implementation; standard integrations; low technical risk
4Achievable with known approaches; some complexity but manageable
3Challenging but possible; requires specialized skills or custom development
2High technical risk; unproven approaches; significant unknowns
1Not currently feasible; technology doesn't exist or won't work in this environment
Key Evidence: API availability confirmed | Data access validated | Integration complexity assessed | Security/compliance requirements understood

4. ORGANIZATIONAL READINESS (1-5)

Will the organization actually adopt this?

ScoreCriteria
5Strong executive sponsorship; users eager to adopt; change management in place
4Good sponsorship; users supportive; standard change management will work
3Mixed signals; some resistance; will require active change management
2Weak sponsorship; significant resistance; adoption at risk
1No sponsorship; active opposition; implementation would likely fail
Key Evidence: Process owner enthusiasm | Executive commitment | User feedback during prototype demo | Historical change adoption patterns

5. IMPLEMENTATION COMPLEXITY (1-5)

How hard will this be to execute?

ScoreCriteria
5Simple implementation; <3 months; minimal disruption; low resource needs
4Moderate complexity; 3-6 months; manageable disruption; reasonable resources
3Complex but achievable; 6-9 months; significant planning required
2Very complex; 9-12 months; high disruption risk; stretched resources
1Extremely complex; 12+ months; would overwhelm organization
Key Evidence: Number of systems involved | Process interdependencies | Resource availability | Competing priorities

6. STRATEGIC VALUE (1-5)

Does this create lasting competitive advantage?

ScoreCriteria
5Transformational; creates significant competitive moat; enables new capabilities
4Strong strategic value; meaningful differentiation; hard to replicate quickly
3Moderate strategic value; efficiency gains but competitors can match
2Tactical only; cost savings without strategic differentiation
1Commodity; everyone will have this; no lasting advantage
Key Evidence: Industry AI maturity (from Phase 1) | Competitor adoption timeline | Customer value impact | Talent/capability building

The "Fund This Implementation If..." Rubric

FUND IF:

  • Prototype demonstrated measurable improvement over manual process
  • At least 3 of 5 opportunities score 20+ individually
  • Total investment required is <20% of Year 1 projected savings
  • Executive sponsor has committed resources and timeline
  • Process owners are advocates, not skeptics
  • Technical architecture uses proven approaches
  • Implementation timeline aligns with other portfolio priorities

DON'T FUND IF:

  • Prototype failed to outperform current process
  • Economics degraded >40% from Phase 1 estimates
  • No clear executive sponsor emerged during Phase 2
  • Process owners are actively resistant
  • Required integrations don't exist or require vendor cooperation
  • Implementation would overwhelm current IT capacity
  • Company is in crisis mode (turnaround, integration, major initiative)

Section B: Opportunity-Level Decision Framework

Scoring and sequencing individual AI opportunities

Individual Opportunity Scoring

For each of the 5 opportunities, score across weighted dimensions:

DimensionWeightDescription
Economic Impact25%Size of savings, ROI strength
Implementation Ease20%Technical complexity, timeline
Prototype Success20%Demo results, user feedback
Organizational Fit15%Sponsor strength, user readiness
Strategic Value10%Competitive advantage created
Risk Level (inverse)10%Lower risk = higher score

Score Interpretation:

  • 4.0+ = Priority 1 (implement in first 100 days)
  • 3.0-3.9 = Priority 2 (implement in days 100-200)
  • 2.0-2.9 = Priority 3 (defer to Year 2 or descope)
  • <2.0 = Do Not Implement (remove from roadmap)

Implementation Sequencing Logic

Sequence Based On:

Quick Wins First - Highest confidence, fastest payback. Build momentum and credibility. Fund subsequent initiatives.
Dependencies Second - Some opportunities enable others. Data infrastructure may need to come first. Integration platforms unlock multiple use cases.
Strategic Bets Third - Higher risk, higher reward. After quick wins proven. Organization has built AI capability.

Sequencing Decision Tree:

START: Does Opportunity A enable Opportunity B? YES -> A must come before B NO -> Continue Does A have higher confidence score than B? YES -> A likely comes first (but check strategic value) NO -> Continue Does A have faster payback than B? YES -> A likely comes first NO -> B likely comes first Is A strategically more important than B? YES -> Consider A first even if harder NO -> Default to easier/faster option

Interview Protocol (Process Owner)

SECTION 1: PROCESS OVERVIEW (10 min) - Walk me through [process] from start to finish - What triggers this process? - What's the end output? - How often does this happen? (volume) SECTION 2: DETAILED WALKTHROUGH (30 min) - Show me how you actually do this (screen share or in-person) - What information do you need to start? - What systems do you use? - Where do you get stuck or slow down? - What are the common exceptions? SECTION 3: PAIN POINTS (15 min) - What's the most frustrating part? - What takes longer than it should? - What errors happen most often? - If you could change one thing, what would it be? SECTION 4: QUANTIFICATION (10 min) - How long does this typically take? - How many do you process per [day/week/month]? - How much of your time goes to this? - What happens when volume spikes? SECTION 5: FUTURE STATE (10 min) - Have you seen how others do this? - What would "great" look like? - What would you do with freed-up time? - Any concerns about automating this?

Quality Standards

Economics Must:

  • Use validated time data (not estimates)
  • Include realization factor (50-70%)
  • Show conservative AND base case
  • Be defensible to CFO scrutiny

Prototype Must:

  • Use real (sample) data
  • Complete end-to-end workflow
  • Be demonstrable in <10 minutes
  • Show clear improvement over manual

Roadmap Must:

  • Be realistic given resources
  • Account for dependencies
  • Include contingency time
  • Have clear success metrics

Profiles Must:

  • Tell complete story
  • Be standalone readable
  • Include actionable recommendation
  • Quantify everything possible
